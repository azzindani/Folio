# Live sweep #25 — turn the engine on its own corpus

Twenty-four passes used synthetic probes: a shape I invented, sent through the
wire, checked. This one uses the **real work** — the 276 designs the project has
accumulated on the server, diagnosed one by one with the check pass 24 had just
added.

    designs: 276
    clean: 269    with errors: 7    threw: 0

Nothing crashed, and 97% is clean. But:

## All seven were sealed

Every one of the 7 is `_mode: complete`. One carries **109** clipped layers.

`seal_design` refuses exactly two things — a blank poster and a blank carousel
page — runs its rescue sweep, and never consults the diagnosis. Live, back to
back on the same file:

    diagnose_design → "1 error(s) + 0 warning(s) to fix."
    seal_design     → status: sealed, remaining: 0,
                      "give the user this link EXACTLY as written"

The last gate before a design is called finished does not ask the engine's own
troubleshooter, and its `next_action` pushes the model toward *sharing*.

**It still seals.** Refusing would strand a blind model holding a design it
cannot finish — `off_canvas` inside a preset group is often not something it can
reach — and pass 17's rule about not "fixing" a load-bearing inconsistency
applies directly. What had to change is the *claim*: `sealed_with_errors`, the
findings in `unresolved`, and a next_action pointing at `diagnose_design` rather
than at the link. Handing over the link is the harm.

The composition of the checks moved to `engine/diagnose-collect.ts` so the gate
and the diagnosis share one answer. Restating that list inside seal would have
been this session's most-repeated mistake.

## Wiring the gate found the second fault immediately

An existing test fixture started failing. Its icon:

| stage | y | canvas |
|---|---|---|
| authored by the model | 180 | 1080 |
| after `add_layers` | 898 | inside |
| **after `seal_design`** | **1095** | **wholly outside** |

De-collide pushes overlapping layers down, and on a full page it can push one
clean off the bottom — at which point the rescue has deleted the content it was
rescuing. `add_layers` has always called `snapOffCanvasContent` afterwards. The
chain `seal_design` runs never did. **Which of its siblings never got told**,
for the ninth time this session.

The snap only fires on a layer with *no* overlap at all, so a deliberate bleed
is untouched — and the test asserts that half too, because a rescue that
flattens intentional bleed would fail §0.4's litmus.

## Reported, not fixed

Re-sealing does **not** repair the 7. Their content overlaps the canvas
*partly* — `x:119 y:1071 w:836 h:82` on a 1080 page, 89% clipped — and the snap
deliberately never touches partial overlap, because that is exactly the shape a
bleed has. Fixing them means moving content inside preset groups, which is a
design decision, not a repair. They now report themselves at seal instead of
passing silently.

## My instrument, a fifth time

I first tallied those designs at 38 / 37 / 33 errors. The real counts are
89 / 96 / 109. `diagnose_design` caps `findings` at 40; I read the array and
believed it complete. **`counts` was honest the whole time** — the tool was
right and my reading was wrong, exactly like the `inspect` scare in pass 24.

A capped list with no marker invites that mistake, so `findings_truncated` now
says what was left out.

Every earlier pass asked the engine a question I had made up. This one asked it
about work it had actually done, and the corpus answered something no synthetic
probe had: not *can* this go wrong, but **how often does it, and does anyone
notice?** 269 times out of 276, nothing was wrong. The other 7 had been wrong
for months, in the open, with a share link attached.

**A gate that does not read the report is not a gate.**
